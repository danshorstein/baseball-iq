# Baseball IQ video production briefs

Give Claude the shared direction below and one lesson brief at a time. The eight core lessons each have a 30-second introduction. Keep the interactive field and quizzes after the video: the video explains the idea, and the app lets a child apply it.

## Shared direction — copy into every request

Create a polished, 30-second, 16:9 paper cut animation for youth baseball players aged 6–14 and their families. Use textured paper, layered shadows, warm cream, field green, navy, red accents, smooth restrained camera movement, and a readable overhead baseball diamond. Make players neutral figures labeled by position, without real names, logos, or uniforms tied to a particular team. Keep large on-screen labels brief and within the central safe area so they remain readable on a phone. Keep the ball, bases, and throw paths easy to follow. Do not show fielders or runners teleporting, colliding, crossing base paths incorrectly, or making an out without the required touch or tag.

Use a warm, clear adult narrator at about 135–145 words per minute. Speak the exact narration supplied, expand position abbreviations naturally (for example, “SS” is “shortstop”), and leave short pauses. Use gentle, upbeat instrumental background music without vocals, licensed for public web use; mix it well below the voice and fade it in and out. Add a restrained ball or glove sound only when it helps explain the play. No autoplay, flashing transitions, or embedded controls.

There are six scenes, each approximately five seconds. Match the voiceover to those scenes. The timing is a production target: prioritize clear narration, and keep the total around 30 seconds. Do not insert new league-specific rules. Rules vary by organization, age, division, and tournament; these videos teach the general defensive idea. Use the described player count only as an example, with no claim that all teams use it.

Deliver an MP4 with H.264 video and AAC audio, 1280×720 or 1920×1080, 24 or 30 fps, yuv420p, and fast-start metadata. Aim for under 8 MB. Also deliver a matching English WebVTT caption file, a JPG poster frame, and the voiceover transcript. The filenames are specified below. Use narration captions rather than extra graphics that obscure the play.

The app already includes draft WebVTT captions timed to six five-second scenes. Retiming those captions to the finished voiceover is part of production. Upload no child-specific information or footage.

## Cover Your Base

Files: cover-your-base.mp4, cover-your-base.vtt, cover-your-base.jpg

Teaching goal: One player fields the ball. Teammates cover the bases and back up the throw. Before the pitch, know which job is yours.

### 0–5 seconds

Visual: Wide overhead paper baseball diamond. All defenders ready. Highlight a baseball.

Voiceover: “Before the pitch, every defender has a job. Start with the ball.”

### 5–10 seconds

Visual: Roll the ball to SS; only SS approaches it.

Voiceover: “A ground ball goes to shortstop. The shortstop fields it.”

### 10–15 seconds

Visual: Move 2B to second and 1B to first. Keep the ball at SS.

Voiceover: “Second base is covered by the second baseman. First base stays ready.”

### 15–20 seconds

Visual: Reset; reverse the ball side. SS covers second while 2B fields.

Voiceover: “If the ball goes to second base, the shortstop covers second.”

### 20–25 seconds

Visual: Three cut-paper cards: BALL, BASE, BACKUP; simple arrows to field roles.

Voiceover: “Choose your job: get the ball, cover a base, or back up.”

### 25–30 seconds

Visual: Freeze diamond; highlight one defender. End card: YOUR TURN.

Voiceover: “Now try the play. Where should you go when the ball moves?”


## Back It Up

Files: back-it-up.mp4, back-it-up.vtt, back-it-up.jpg

Teaching goal: A backup gets behind the intended receiver, along the throw’s path. That gives the defense another chance if the throw gets away.

### 0–5 seconds

Visual: Paper diamond; arrow from SS to first, ball sailing just past receiver.

Voiceover: “A good defense plans for the throw that gets away.”

### 5–10 seconds

Visual: RF moves toward foul territory behind first, along the throw line.

Voiceover: “On a throw to first, right field moves behind the receiver.”

### 10–15 seconds

Visual: Ball rolls to RF. Label: SECOND CHANCE.

Voiceover: “If the ball gets past first, the backup stops it.”

### 15–20 seconds

Visual: Two separate vignettes: LF behind third, P behind catcher.

Voiceover: “On other throws, teammates back up third base or home plate.”

### 20–25 seconds

Visual: Show receiver, throw line, and backup separated clearly, with a small space arrow.

Voiceover: “The best spot is behind the throw, with room to react.”

### 25–30 seconds

Visual: Rules card beside diamond; runner stops as backup returns the ball.

Voiceover: “Your league sets the runner rules. Your job is preventing extra bases.”


## Get It In

Files: get-it-in.mp4, get-it-in.vtt, get-it-in.jpg

Teaching goal: An outfielder gets the ball back to the infield through a cutoff or relay. A teammate backs up the outfielder while the other defenders cover bases.

### 0–5 seconds

Visual: Base hit to LF; fielders begin distinct roles.

Voiceover: “The ball reaches the outfield. The whole defense moves to help.”

### 5–10 seconds

Visual: LF fields, CF backs up. Use one center fielder to avoid implying ten players.

Voiceover: “One outfielder gets the ball. A nearby teammate backs up.”

### 10–15 seconds

Visual: SS aligns between LF and second. Raise simple paper-cut arms.

Voiceover: “The cutoff player becomes a clear target between ball and play.”

### 15–20 seconds

Visual: Ball travels from LF to SS. Avoid drawing an inevitable out.

Voiceover: “Make a controlled throw to the cutoff instead of a desperate heave.”

### 20–25 seconds

Visual: Ball farther out; relay moves deeper, then returns toward the infield.

Voiceover: “For a deeper hit, a relay helps bring the ball back in.”

### 25–30 seconds

Visual: Catcher communication symbol; bases covered. End card: GET IT IN.

Voiceover: “Listen to your teammates. Get the ball in and protect the bases.”


## Hold the Ball

Files: hold-the-ball.mp4, hold-the-ball.vtt, hold-the-ball.jpg

Teaching goal: A throw needs a purpose. If a runner is already safe and there is no realistic out, secure the ball and stay ready rather than making a late throw.

### 0–5 seconds

Visual: Infielder holds ball; runners are visibly on their bases.

Voiceover: “Having the ball does not mean you must throw it.”

### 5–10 seconds

Visual: Decision card: IS THERE A PLAY? Show batter already at first.

Voiceover: “First ask: is there a realistic chance to get an out?”

### 10–15 seconds

Visual: Hypothetical dotted wild throw; runner considers another base, no guaranteed outcome.

Voiceover: “If the runner is already safe, a late throw may only add risk.”

### 15–20 seconds

Visual: Solid ball with fielder; eyes symbol toward runner; teammates remain ready.

Voiceover: “Secure the ball, watch the runners, and stay ready for another play.”

### 20–25 seconds

Visual: LIVE BALL badge remains visible; umpire and local rules card.

Voiceover: “Holding the ball does not automatically make it dead. Follow your rules.”

### 25–30 seconds

Visual: Two clear options: REAL OUT and NO PLAY: HOLD. End card: YOUR TURN.

Voiceover: “Make the next smart decision. Take a real out, or hold it.”


## Force or Tag?

Files: force-or-tag.mp4, force-or-tag.vtt, force-or-tag.jpg

Teaching goal: Look at the runners and the bases behind them. A force lets a defender touch the base; without a force, the defender generally needs to tag the runner.

### 0–5 seconds

Visual: Runner on first and batter; arrows toward first and second.

Voiceover: “Before you throw, ask: does this runner have to advance?”

### 5–10 seconds

Visual: Highlight occupied first, batter approaching first, and runner advancing.

Voiceover: “With a runner on first, the batter makes that runner go to second.”

### 10–15 seconds

Visual: Defender at second with ball arrives before runner. Label: FORCE.

Voiceover: “For that force out, hold the ball and touch second in time.”

### 15–20 seconds

Visual: Reset: first empty, second occupied. Highlight empty first.

Voiceover: “With only a runner on second, there is no force at third.”

### 20–25 seconds

Visual: Defender’s glove with ball touches runner, distinct from touching the bag.

Voiceover: “To get that runner out at third, tag the runner with the ball.”

### 25–30 seconds

Visual: Batter out badge; advancing force arrows disappear. End card: FORCE OR TAG?

Voiceover: “A caught ball or another out can remove a force. Read the play.”


## Call It!

Files: call-it.mp4, call-it.vtt, call-it.jpg

Teaching goal: Communicate early so one player handles the ball and the others can help. These examples use the closest defender; your coach may also teach a fly-ball priority system.

### 0–5 seconds

Visual: Gap ball between two outfielders, separated with room to move.

Voiceover: “Two defenders can reach the ball. Clear communication keeps the play organized.”

### 5–10 seconds

Visual: Highlight closest fielder; speech bubble: I GOT IT!

Voiceover: “In this example, the closer player calls: I got it!”

### 10–15 seconds

Visual: Second fielder changes route behind, avoiding a collision.

Voiceover: “The teammate hears the call and moves behind to back up.”

### 15–20 seconds

Visual: Infield pop-up; caller highlighted; other players give space.

Voiceover: “On a pop-up, listen to the call and your coach’s priority system.”

### 20–25 seconds

Visual: One ball-handler; others cover bases or back up.

Voiceover: “One player catches or fields. Everyone else still has a job.”

### 25–30 seconds

Visual: Three icons: TALK, WATCH, HELP. End card: CALL IT.

Voiceover: “Use a loud voice, keep watching the ball, and help your teammate.”


## Runner on Third

Files: runner-on-third.mp4, runner-on-third.vtt, runner-on-third.jpg

Teaching goal: A runner on third makes the decision more important. Read the runner, the number of outs, and how much time you have. A fast grounder and a slowly fielded ball can call for different plays.

### 0–5 seconds

Visual: Runner on third, batter ready, infielder checks both.

Voiceover: “A runner on third changes what you watch before the pitch.”

### 5–10 seconds

Visual: Cards: OUTS, RUNNER, TIME. Keep legal details neutral.

Voiceover: “Think about the outs, the runner, and the time for a throw.”

### 10–15 seconds

Visual: Fielder secures quick grounder; realistic throw window toward first.

Voiceover: “A quickly fielded grounder may give you a sure out at first.”

### 15–20 seconds

Visual: Batter reaches first before fielder is ready.

Voiceover: “A slow play may leave the batter safe before you can throw.”

### 20–25 seconds

Visual: Fielder holds and looks toward third; runner returns to base.

Voiceover: “Then watch the runner and avoid a late throw with no purpose.”

### 25–30 seconds

Visual: Two contrasted play diagrams: quick fielding, slow fielding. End card: READ THE PLAY.

Voiceover: “There is no answer for every grounder. Read this play and choose.”


## It’s Hit to Me!

Files: hit-to-me.mp4, hit-to-me.vtt, hit-to-me.jpg

Teaching goal: Prepare before the pitch. If you field the ball, know your likely next play and who will cover the base. Adjust that plan when the ball or runners change the situation.

### 0–5 seconds

Visual: Player in ready position; thought bubble with ball and base.

Voiceover: “Before the pitch, ask: if it comes to me, what is my play?”

### 5–10 seconds

Visual: Comebacker; P fields, 1B covers, RF backs up.

Voiceover: “A grounder to the pitcher often means a controlled throw to first.”

### 10–15 seconds

Visual: C fields near plate; P moves to home, first ready.

Voiceover: “A slow roller to the catcher can mean the pitcher covers home.”

### 15–20 seconds

Visual: 1B fields near bag and carries ball to touch base.

Voiceover: “If first base fields near the bag, stepping on first may work.”

### 20–25 seconds

Visual: 1B off bag; P covers first and receives toss.

Voiceover: “If first base moves away, the pitcher may cover for the throw.”

### 25–30 seconds

Visual: All defenders ready. End card: THINK BEFORE THE PITCH.

Voiceover: “Know your team’s plan, then adjust to the ball and the runners.”

## Add the finished assets

Place the MP4, VTT, and JPG together in cubs-baseball-iq/public/videos/. The lesson ID is the filename. Run npm run build:pages and commit the rebuilt root index.html plus videos/ when using branch-based Pages hosting, or let the included Pages workflow build and publish them. Restart the development server after adding a new MP4 so it discovers the new filename. Missing videos show a written intro; a failed video shows the transcript and lets the child continue.

The app does not require watching to the end. Playback has ordinary browser controls, captions, no autoplay, and a transcript. MP4s stay separate from the HTML bundle and load only on their lesson.
