# Rules, age groups, and competition types

Rec, advanced/select, and travel describe a competition format. They do not prescribe one national rulebook. A travel team may play events from multiple organizations; each event may add local rules. Age eligibility also does not guarantee the same pitching or base distances.

The user’s current team is Ponte Vedra 10U Sharks; the previous team was Julington Creek 9U rec. Their division and season rulebooks were not supplied or verified. This app deliberately provides no claimed official preset for either team. Ask each coach or league for the division rules and the tournament supplements, then save the rulebook name/link in Setup and share the exported configuration.

## What may differ

| Rule or setting | What to compare | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bats | USA Baseball, USSSA certification, BBCOR where applicable, diameter/drop and banned models | Legal equipment differs by organization and age; a logo alone may not resolve every restriction. |
| Pitching | Coach/machine/player format, pitch counts or innings, rest, pitcher reentry | Preparation, catcher responsibilities, and player availability change. |
| Leading and stealing | No leads, release point, steal restrictions, stealing home | Runners and middle infielders must anticipate different actions. |
| Uncaught third strike | Enabled or disabled; first-base occupancy and outs | The batter may require a tag or throw to first instead of being automatically retired. |
| Infield fly | Enabled or disabled, relevant occupied bases and outs | An umpire’s call can retire the batter and remove the force. |
| Balks and pickoffs | Whether enforced, warnings, division exceptions | Pitcher behavior and runner management change. |
| Field layout | Base paths, pitching distance, nine/ten defenders | Coverage and realistic throw distances change. |
| Overthrows | Live versus dead ball, local advancement caps, out-of-play awards | Recovering a loose ball and stopping extra bases remain important. |
| Ending play | Umpire calls time versus special local control rules | A defender yelling “time” should not be taught as universally stopping play. |
| Games and lineups | Innings, time limits, mercy rules, run caps, continuous batting, substitutions and minimum play | Strategy and who can play where change. |

Official organization references to locate the applicable rulebook: [Little League playing rules](https://www.littleleague.org/playing-rules/), [USSSA Baseball](https://www.usssabaseball.org/), and [Babe Ruth League](https://www.baberuthleague.org/). These links are references for finding rules, not evidence that a specific local team follows them.

## What the app actually changes

- Age templates load a suggested training setup, including fielders and rule switches. Switching age replaces those rules; review any local overrides again.
- Nine-player setups have LF, CF, and RF. Ten-player setups have LF, LCF, RCF, and RF. Scenarios, questions, movement, labels, and backup assignments use the selected layout.
- The live-overthrow example can show additional advancement beyond second; the locally capped example stops at second. These are authored illustrations, not a prediction of how many bases a real runner will earn.
- Leading, stealing, uncaught third strike, and infield fly settings change the rule notes and rule-quiz answers. They do not yet add full stolen-base or strike-three animations.
- Pitching format changes the pitch origin in the illustration. Base path length is recorded and displayed; the schematic field is normalized, not a scale/timing simulation.
- Contact strength changes starting outfield depth and can change which outfielder is closest in a gap-ball example. It does not change legal rules or imply runner speed.
- Competition type describes the team. Changing “rec” to “travel” does not silently select a rulebook.

## Defensive principles and tactical choices

Coverage, communication, and backups are transferable ideas. Specific cutoff assignments, fly-ball priority, whether to play in, and which out to attempt are coaching choices. The app uses one understandable defensive system; a coach may teach a different sound system.

The third-base hold lesson now specifies a slowly fielded ball where the batter is already safe. A promptly fielded grounder can allow a real out at first. Choosing between those plays depends on the runner, outs, score, timing, and defender ability; this app does not model all of those factors yet.

Force logic handles occupied-base chains and forces removed by a retired batter or trailing runner. More advanced cases—appeals, interference, obstruction, scoring on third outs, and complex multi-out plays—need separate scenarios and validation before being taught here.
